Black Forest MSO, Inc.

Entity & payment structure

A proposed structure that keeps the MSO, physician-owned telehealth entity, and licensed pharmacy operationally distinct while preserving a single Black Forest customer experience.

Proposed planning model · August 2026

1. Entity structure

The MSO controls non-clinical infrastructure. Clinical and dispensing decisions stay with the licensed entities.

NON-CLINICALBlack Forest MSO, Inc.

Brand, domain, software, marketing, support, billing infrastructure, admin operations.

→
CLINICALPhysician-Owned Telehealth PC

Clinical evaluation, diagnosis, prescribing and medical decision-making.

→
DISPENSINGLicensed 503A Pharmacy

Prescription validation, compounding, dispensing and shipment.

2. $199 patient payment

Proposed economic allocation for one completed order.

Telehealth PC

$10
Medical / clinical fee

Charged through the telehealth entity's own Stripe account and deposited to its bank account.

Pharmacy / RX

$19
Prescription / dispensing component

Charged through the pharmacy's own Stripe account and deposited to the pharmacy.

Black Forest MSO

$170
Non-clinical platform / admin component

Only to the extent supported by bona fide non-clinical services and approved by healthcare counsel.

Important: The $170 MSO component should be documented as payment for legitimate non-clinical services—not compensation for referrals, prescribing, or dispensing. State fee-splitting and corporate-practice rules should be reviewed before launch.

3. Stripe architecture

Three legal entities, three merchant accounts, three matching bank accounts.

Stripe #1 — MSO

Merchant: Black Forest MSO, Inc.

Receives: $170 proposed MSO component

Deposit: MSO bank account

Stripe #2 — Telehealth

Merchant: Physician-owned PC

Receives: $10 clinical fee

Deposit: PC bank account

Stripe #3 — Pharmacy

Merchant: Licensed pharmacy

Receives: $19 RX/dispensing fee

Deposit: Pharmacy bank account

4. Customer experience

One Black Forest checkout

The customer can remain inside one branded flow while the backend creates entity-specific charges. The payment method can be tokenized so the customer does not need to manually enter the card multiple times.

5. Centralized banking / treasury

If California PC, New York PC, and other required state professional entities are genuinely separate legal entities with different EINs, each should generally have its own account — but they can all be managed inside one bank and one treasury portal.

ONE BANK ONE TREASURY LOGIN ├── Hub PC account ├── CA PC account ├── NY PC account ├── TX entity account ├── NJ entity account └── other required professional entities ↓ automatic sweep CENTRAL TREASURY ACCOUNT
Operationally: this can feel like one account while preserving legal separation. Banks support multi-entity treasury structures, zero-balance accounts, cash concentration and automated sweeps. Intercompany transfers should be properly recorded on each entity's books.
